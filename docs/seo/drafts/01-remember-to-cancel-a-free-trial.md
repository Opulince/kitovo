# How to remember to cancel a free trial before you're charged

Free trials are easy to start and easy to forget. Signing up takes a minute. The day the trial turns into a paid charge comes days or weeks later, usually when you're thinking about something else.

You don't need willpower to fix that. You need the charge date written down somewhere, and a reminder early enough to act on. Here's how.

## 1. Find the charge date the day you sign up

The day you start a trial is the day you know the most about it. Look for the date in:

- the "your trial has started" confirmation email
- the sign-up or checkout screen
- the billing or subscription page in the service's account settings

If you can't work out the exact day, use the earliest date the service could charge you. Being early is safe; being late is not.

## 2. Write down the price and how to cancel

When the reminder arrives, you want to decide in seconds, not go hunting. Note:

- **What you'll pay** once the trial ends.
- **How to cancel:** in the app, on the website, or through Google Play if that's where you signed up.

## 3. Get more than one reminder

A single reminder is easy to swipe away and forget. Schedule at least two: one a few days before, so there's time to think, and one close to the charge, as a last call.

## 4. Decide, then act straight away

When the reminder comes, make the call there and then. If you're keeping it, you're done. If you're not, cancel with the service right away instead of saving it for later.

## What a reminder can't do

A reminder tells you in time. It doesn't act for you:

- **Cancelly doesn't cancel subscriptions.** You cancel with the service you signed up for.
- **Reminders arrive by email,** so check your spam or promotions folder for the first one, and mark it "not spam" if it landed there.
- **Reminders follow the date you add.** If the date is wrong, edit the trial and the reminders move with it.
